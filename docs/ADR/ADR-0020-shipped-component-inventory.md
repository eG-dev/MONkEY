# ADR-0020 ORT-Assisted Third-Party Notices

_Creation date_: 2026-10-18

_Updated_: 2026-10-25

_Consolidated_: ADR-0021 merged into this record by explicit approval on
2026-10-18. The earlier automated approach is retained under Alternatives.

## Context

Recipients need the license, copyright and NOTICE material required for software
and assets redistributed with POCKET MONkEY. Direct dependency manifests and image
scans alone do not establish that scope: browser/server bundles, transitive and
native dependencies, copied assets and runtime/OS components all matter.
Automated identification provides evidence but cannot decide attribution,
compatibility, rights or source obligations. Mandatory archive reconciliation on
every build adds maintenance without replacing that review. External package
links alone do not satisfy obligations requiring accompanying material or offline
access.

A useful human-facing About list is not a complete dependency inventory.
Manually cataloging every transitive dependency duplicates automated discovery
and creates disproportionate maintenance. Distribution obligations concern the
material delivered to recipients, not the number of individually curated UI
entries.

## Decision

- Use the OSS Review Toolkit (ORT) for automated dependency and license discovery, including direct and transitive npm/NuGet dependencies. Review exceptions and relevant changes rather than manually cataloging the entire graph. Do not maintain a competing bespoke inventory or archive-reconciliation implementation. Evaluate and pin the ORT version and invocation against the replacement projects before requiring it in CI; hosting, configuration and reporting details remain implementation work.
- Maintain explicit component, About-entry and document mappings in a versioned `catalog.json`, with unmodified original documents in its sibling `originals/` directory. Record exact identities/versions, evidence, provenance and document hashes. Generate the canonical root [THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.txt](../../THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.txt) deterministically from those reviewed inputs and commit it for source recipients; do not hand-edit a second combined bundle. This curated input is not a complete dependency graph or a competing discovery engine. Original notices may be collected manually; ORT downloading, scanning and notice generation are optional capabilities to evaluate, not prerequisites for adoption.
- Use the exact shipped package or matching upstream release/revision as the primary source of license, copyright and NOTICE material. Preserve supplied texts unchanged with provenance. Reasonably rely on upstream-provided information unless there are obvious gaps or contradictions; do not routinely audit upstream authorship or reconstruct its compliance process. Check applicability to the actual component/version and bundled material, record missing or conflicting information as unresolved findings, and do not invent notices or assume one project's license covers all its dependencies. This proportional review approach does not establish legal completeness or waive distribution obligations.
- Deliver notices incrementally. Start with a handcrafted, reviewed set for Angular, .NET, ASP.NET Core, Entity Framework Core, SQLite, Taiga UI, Lucide and Roboto, identifying the actual shipped packages/assets and versions and retaining their required original texts and attributions. Include the SQLite provider/binding/native bundle chain, not only the engine name; distinguish the SQLite engine's public-domain status from the separate terms of its wrappers and packaging. This set and its About presentation do not depend on ORT. Mark an unfinished curated collection as partial, without equating its eventual completion with a complete dependency inventory or distribution review.
- Separate accepted curated-collection completion from release review. The catalog's `coverage` records whether the agreed collection is `partial` or `complete`; a completed collection uses About's existing state without a partial warning. Its required `releaseReview` remains `pending` until the distribution review is performed and explicitly recorded as `reviewed`. Release validation requires both a completed collection and reviewed release status, as well as resolved component findings. Do not manufacture release approval from collection acceptance or scanner success.
- Keep About's individually curated entries focused on direct application dependencies, selected frameworks/assets and significant separately shipped backend/native components. Do not require an individual About entry, manual catalog record or manual original-file download for every transitive or OS package. Required material may instead accompany the distribution in reviewed upstream notice bundles, package license files or generated notices; preserve its applicability and recipient access without duplicating every component in the UI. Keep the canonical root bundle for the curated inputs and preserve supplemental upstream material alongside it as appropriate.
- Use later ORT-assisted discovery and supplemental asset/runtime checks to identify gaps, not to create an exhaustive manual collection backlog. Target missing/conflicting license or notice material, changed terms, separately shipped native code and applicable copyleft/source obligations. Add curated entries where useful, but resolve required distribution material regardless of its UI representation. Staging the overview does not defer obligations already applicable to distributed material: satisfy those before distribution, preserve accompanying upstream notices, or withhold the affected artifact until resolved.
- Review ORT's coverage against redistributed browser/server packages and transitives, backend/native dependencies, fonts/artwork, and container/runtime software. Record gaps and supplement the overview where necessary; package-manager analysis alone does not prove complete redistribution coverage. Distinguish actual delivery from build-only tools and metadata-only dependencies. Include redistributed native payloads regardless of execution platform; their presence does not establish platform support.
- Preserve required component-specific copyright and attribution notices. Shared identical license texts may appear once with explicit component associations. Retain applicable NOTICE material; package/source links supplement rather than replace local text.
- Store reviewed originals for all currently cataloged packages, runtimes, engines and assets under their respective `MAJOR.x` folders. Assume licensing stays unchanged across minor and patch releases within each major series; routine updates do not require repeat downloads or duplicate originals. Keep exact shipped component versions and the captured document's real upstream URL, revision and hash. The folder is a storage convention, not a wildcard version or source revision. Review a new major series before reusing its documents. Revisit reuse when upstream announces changed terms or obvious contradictory material appears; this trust does not automatically cover separate uncataloged dependencies or replace component-specific notices.
- Assemble the combined document and static About data/local documents from the same maintained inputs. Ordinary development/PR builds package the maintained notices without enforcing dependency evidence, document hashes, or assembled-bundle consistency. Enforce those consistency checks for release builds, alongside the independent release-review gate; explicit manual checks remain available. Capture the catalog and original documents in prepared inputs, adding validated dependency/asset evidence for releases. Maintain originals/mappings and the generated root distribution output in source control, not generated web assets. Do not download notices at runtime or introduce consent state, backend endpoints or a separate compliance engine. Later discovery adapters propose changes to this same input contract without silently replacing reviewed documents or About grouping.
- Review the list and notice document when shipped dependencies/assets/runtime inputs change and before each release. Include operating-system changes even when mutable package repositories change without a source edit. Record reviewed inputs, findings and unresolved items. Resolve attribution, compatibility choices, rights and applicable source/source-offer/relinking obligations explicitly; notices do not satisfy every license obligation by themselves.
- Use ORT reports, package/build metadata and targeted initial/release audits to assist the review. Keep analysis separate from ordinary application builds until its reproducible invocation and coverage are verified. Do not require bespoke NuGet archive parsing, emitted-byte inventory, full-image scanning/reconciliation, or their parser dependencies in every development/CI build. Preserve upstream runtime and OS notices already supplied in the image. Do not modify or rebuild the tested artifact for publication.
- Distinguish source-only public development from published Docker images and versioned releases. A public development repository does not require completed ORT setup, comprehensive dependency/asset reporting or About integration. It still requires rights to publish the included source/assets and retention of applicable license, copyright and NOTICE material; repository visibility does not waive those obligations.
- Defer ORT setup and the release dependency/asset review to final release preparation, after source-only public development can begin. A curated notice set/About presentation may precede that tooling and need not grow into an exhaustive inventory. Keep incomplete distribution material explicitly marked as draft. Ordinary development builds may use it, but public image/release distribution requires the applicable accompanying notices, resolved review findings, applicable source delivery and the independent release gates. Neither a complete direct-package list nor successful generation, copying or scanning is legal approval.

This refines [build identity and About assets](ADR-0019-build-identity-and-about-assets.md)
and [GitHub delivery](ADR-0006-github-delivery.md), without changing application
identity or project-license generation. Operational review guidance is maintained
in the [build guide](../development.md#third-party-notices).

## Alternatives

- Manifest-based npm tooling can retain development-only packages and cannot prove which bundled or copied material ships.
- Image-only scanning misses bundled npm identities and NuGet notice material. Requiring a DLL for each NuGet identity also loses native and metadata-only packages.
- Mandatory hybrid collection combining Angular emitted-byte statistics, locked NuGet archive/publish verification and a digest-pinned final-image scan was evaluated. It provides stronger input evidence but adds parser dependencies, binary reconciliation and image-export complexity without replacing human notice/source review. Prefer optional initial/release audits over this machinery in every build.
- External package/source links alone do not retain required local license, copyright or NOTICE material.
- Hosted SCA services were considered. ORT was selected explicitly; automated retrieval of original notice files is not a selection requirement.
